# Mucke examples

Open a `.mucke` file in the app. Each file loops until you remove its `$name` lines or close the app. Sample paths use `../samples/` because they are resolved relative to this folder.

- [`everything_means_nothing_to_me.mucke`](everything_means_nothing_to_me.mucke): a verse lead-in and three bars of the piano chorus in F-sharp major, with separate right and left hands. The 75 BPM, eighth-note arrangement is adapted from the [score supplied for this project](https://tabs.ultimate-guitar.com/tab/elliott-smith/everything-means-nothing-to-me-official-6232580).
- [`here_comes_the_sun.mucke`](here_comes_the_sun.mucke): the four-bar A–A–D–E intro progression in concert pitch, with picked guitar and a supporting bass. See [Alan Pollack's song analysis](https://beatlesnotes.fyi/note-hcts).
- [`lift.mucke`](lift.mucke): a B–A–G-sharp-minor–E loop with guitar, bass, kick, and snare. The chords follow a [Lift chord chart](https://chordier.com/lift-chords/); the drum part is an added demo layer.
- [`twinkle_twinkle.mucke`](twinkle_twinkle.mucke): the complete public-domain melody on a music-box synth.

These are short looping arrangements, not complete recordings. Each pattern step has one fixed duration, and synth notes stop at the next step. Longer notes in the originals are repeated here rather than held. `8/8` gives the Elliott Smith, Beatles, and Radiohead examples an eighth-note grid while keeping their four-quarter-note bars. Numbered `$name` lines connect instruments to the volume sliders.
